Research

Current work.

Project lead · In progress

Robust underwater manipulation in dynamic ocean environments

With Prof. Mark Cutkosky and Prof. Shuran Song, Stanford University

An affordance-conditioned diffusion policy that learns underwater grasping from data collected on land, with no underwater teleoperation. It plans from monocular depth rather than RGB, because an air-to-water shift moves a DINOv2 feature by 0.166 in RGB and 0.003 in depth. In distribution it grasps 17 of 20; under a background shift it holds 16 of 20 where the RGB baseline scores zero. In the field at Hopkins Marine Station the same policy fell below 5%, because nothing in its state told it a current was pushing it. I am rebuilding the stack around that: stereo depth, a SAM2-based relative-pose estimator, a platform-independent relative end-effector action space, and MPC with a disturbance observer.

Side project · In progress

PowerBuoy sensor deployment

With Civil Engineering faculty at Stanford and the Naval Postgraduate School

Oceanographic sensors reach a moored buoy only once it has been brought to the surface, or when a diver attaches them to the mooring cable by hand. The project asks whether a robot can do that work instead.

Contributor · Submitted to T-RO

Whisker-based hydrodynamic sensing

BDML · Advisor: Prof. Mark Cutkosky

A tactile sensor built on a fiber Bragg grating and shaped like a harbor seal vibrissa, which reads the trail of disturbed water a moving source leaves behind. On an ROV, a single whisker chose the correct branch of a delayed wake in 17 of 20 trials from whisker signals alone. My contribution was the experimental platform design, the signal-processing pipeline, and the underwater field testing. An ongoing extension distributes several multiplexed whiskers around one vehicle to recover flow direction, vortex location, wake geometry, and contact discrimination.

Publications & talks

Published work.

Journal · under review Submitted to T-RO · 2026

Fiber Bragg Grating Whiskers for Bioinspired Hydrodynamic Perception on Underwater Robots

Hao Li*, Tianyu Tu, Siyue Yao, Ziyang Chang, Juhyun Jung, Xiaochi Xie, Long Yin Chung, Tian-Ao Ren, Genliang Chen, Mark R. Cutkosky*

Compact FBG whiskers with a non-uniform taper and elliptical cross-section, modeled on harbor seal vibrissae. Controlled towing experiments show a monotonic relative-flow response from 0.1–0.6 m/s. In the field, a single front-mounted whisker let a small ROV select the correct branch in 17 of 20 trials (85.0%) from whisker signals alone. I contributed the experimental platform design, signal-processing pipeline, and underwater field testing.
