Journal · under review Submitted to T-RO · 2026 arXiv:2608.24724

Fiber Bragg Grating Whiskers for Bioinspired Hydrodynamic Perception on Underwater Robots

Hao Li*, Tianyu Tu, Siyue Yao, Ziyang Chang, Juhyun Jung, Xiaochi Xie, Long Yin Chung, Tian-Ao Ren, Genliang Chen, Mark R. Cutkosky*
*Corresponding authors. Stanford University · Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Tsinghua University.

Harbor seals track hydrodynamic trails with their vibrissae, which gives them passive perception of moving targets in dark or turbid water. The paper presents compact fiber Bragg grating (FBG) whiskers for underwater robots, built with the same non-uniform taper and elliptical cross-section as the biological original.

Controlled towing experiments show a monotonic response to relative flow from 0.1–0.6 m/s, a strong reduction in self-induced oscillation compared with a cylindrical baseline, and a pronounced dependence on angle of attack. In a field deployment, a single front-mounted whisker let a small ROV distinguish continuing straight from executing a turn, correctly selecting the branch in 17 of 20 trials (85.0%) from whisker signals alone.

I designed the experimental platform, wrote the signal-processing pipeline, and ran the underwater field testing. The paper is submitted to T-RO and awaiting a decision. Field footage from the two ocean results, and the project in full, are on the whisker page.
